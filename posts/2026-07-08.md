Today I worked on my project involving learning about mechanistic interpretability. I am starting from basically ground zero, with a very high level understanding of how LLMs work and almost nothing else. So I've been working with Claude along with Neel Nanda videos to walk me through how mechanistic interpretability works. So far, I have learned about how a model gets to its output -- it goes through layers, each layer having multiple heads running in parallel, starting from layer 0 and in the case of GPT-2 exiting at layer 12. At each layer the model does higher level reasoning, and each head is responsible for their own specified task. 

I started with a basic IOI (Indirect Object Identification) test: "When John and Mary went to the store, John gave a drink to ___." Then I performed a DLA (Direct Logit Attribution) to see what heads were making the prediction "Mary." From there, I found two interesting things: 

1. A different head was used to predict a pronoun object then a name object (ie. she vs. Mary)

2. That same head was used when I ablated the model and removed the heads that predicted "Mary" so that the model predicted "them"

From these I was able to deduce that there are two kinds of reasoning circuits for an IOI of this structure. One where it uses the subjects and sentence structure to reason the next word is probably a pronoun, and another where it copies the seen name. And this corresponds with the definition of layers. The heads that predicted pronouns were at a higher layer than the heads that predicted proper nouns. Which makes sense, as deducing the correct pronoun to use requires much higher level thinking than predicting a before seen proper noun. 

Overall, I am having a lot of fun playing around with IOI and seeing how GPT-2 behaves.
